Limitations

Last updated 17 October 2026

Pharvel is not medical advice. It shows what US FDA drug labels and FDA tables say. Always ask a pharmacist or prescriber before starting, stopping or changing a medicine. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

What Pharvel does

  • Finds each medicine you name (brand or generic) using NIH RxNorm.
  • Quotes the passage in each FDA label that mentions the other medicine or its drug class, with a link to the label.
  • Adds a fixed plain-language summary, the enzyme mechanism listed by FDA where one applies, and how many FDA adverse-event reports list both medicines.

What it does not cover

  • Doses — it never gives or adjusts doses.
  • Your situation — pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, older age, kidney or liver disease, allergies and genetics can all change the risk. Labels have separate warnings for these that this check does not apply.
  • Supplements, herbal products and foods — only where an FDA label or FDA table names them.
  • Medicines outside the US — products and labels in other countries can differ.
  • Completeness — labels do not list every interaction. "Nothing found" means the labels checked do not mention it; it does not mean the combination is safe.

How well it works

Pharvel is tested against reference drug pairs with known answers, including a held-out set written before its first run, and compared with an independent research database of drug interactions. Across three separate comparisons, Pharvel flagged 82–86% of the interactions that database rates as major, so roughly 1 in 6 serious interactions was shown as "nothing found". This is the main reason a "nothing found" result must never be taken as safe. The reference sets have not yet been reviewed by a pharmacist.

Severity labels

"Contraindicated" and "Serious warning" come from the label's contraindication and boxed-warning sections or serious wording; "Interaction documented" means the label mentions the combination; "Class-level mention" means the label names the drug class, not the medicine. These follow label wording, which is not a clinical severity scale.

Something wrong?

Use "Report a problem with this answer" under any result. Reports are reviewed weekly; serious errors the same day.
